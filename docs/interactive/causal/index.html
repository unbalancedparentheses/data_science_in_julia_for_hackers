What changed?

The population and its potential sales stay fixed. Only the historical pricing rule changes. Comparing busy days with quiet days mixes the price effect with demand. Adjustment compares prices within each demand group, then weights both contrasts by their share of the population.

The randomized comparison uses a separate, fixed 50/50 assignment. Its small sampling error need not be zero. A narrow interval around the naive estimate measures sampling variability; it cannot remove confounding.

This is a synthetic world of 4,000 observations where we know raising the price reduces every observation’s sales by 20 units. Demand is measured before pricing, both prices occur in both groups, and there are no other common causes or spillovers. Those assumptions make adjustment meaningful here; a real pricing spreadsheet cannot establish them for us.

Try it: move to 50%. Why do the naive and adjusted estimates now nearly agree? Then move to 95% and inspect the small comparison groups.
