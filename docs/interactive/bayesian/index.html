How much should the prior matter?

Our football team has played 80 matches. We expect about two goals per match—but how firmly should we believe that before seeing the scores?

Predict first: strengthening a prior with the same mean changes its spread. Will it make zero goals more or less likely? Does seeing 80 matches change your answer?

A confident model can still miss the game

The prior is Gamma(shape = 2s, rate = s), so its mean is always 2. With s equivalent matches of exposure, the posterior adds the observed goals to the shape and the 80 matches to the rate. This exposure interpretation describes the conjugate update, not a claim that we actually observed those prior matches.

We integrate over the uncertain scoring rate to obtain exact Gamma–Poisson predictive probabilities. The chart includes both scoring randomness and rate uncertainty. It is a distribution for one future match, not an interval for a whole season’s zero-goal fraction.

The synthetic schedule hides a twist: 40 difficult opponents have rate 0.2 and 40 easy opponents have rate 4. Pooling all matches into one rate misses this heterogeneity. The orange bars make that mismatch visible. Training frequencies are a descriptive check, not independent validation.
