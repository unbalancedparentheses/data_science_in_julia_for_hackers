What did we assume?

We use the chapter’s illustrative ranges [25.94, 38.84, 52.81, 45.54, 17.24] metres and times [3.91, 4.57, 5.43, 4.85, 3.15] seconds. Gravity has a uniform grid prior from 0.02 to 10 m/s² in steps of 0.02. We average over 61 equally weighted midpoint angle cells, or one cell when the angle is known.

All throws share the same uncertain angle. Range errors are independent Normal errors with the chosen standard deviation. Times are exact, launch and landing heights match, and drag is absent. Reducing the noise slider asserts a more precise instrument; it does not improve measurements already taken.

Each grid point predicts range as g × time² / (2 tan(angle)). We normalize its likelihood after subtracting the largest log likelihood, then sum over angles. Escape speed is 11 √(g / 19.6) km/s, using the chapter’s approximate Earth/Mars radius ratio. The chart accounts for the unequal speed widths of the transformed gravity cells.

The result excludes propulsion failures, atmosphere, uncertain radius, and clock bias. More throws will not cure a shared calibration error. A lower speed budget changes the decision probability; it does not change what the stones tell us about gravity.
